The 4 Biggest Test Traps

Teachers love putting these 4 traps on 6th-grade comma tests. Learn how to spot them instantly:

Trap #1: The "Fake FANBOYS" (Compound Predicate)

Only put a comma before and / but / so if there is a second subject after it!

✓ Correct: Leo ran down the field, and he kicked the ball. (Two subjects: Leo + he)
✗ Wrong: Leo ran down the field, and kicked the ball. (No subject after 'and' = NO COMMA!)
Trap #2: The Dreaded "Comma Splice"

A comma is too weak to glue two complete sentences together by itself without a FANBOYS word!

✗ Comma Splice: The storm knocked out the power, we lit candles.
✓ Fixed: The storm knocked out the power, so we lit candles.
Trap #3: The "One-Comma" Interrupter Mistake

When a nonessential clause or appositive is in the middle of a sentence, it acts like a pair of handles—you need BOTH commas (opening and closing), never just one!

✓ Correct: Mrs. Davis, our science teacher, loves astronomy.
Trap #4: Trailing Comma in Dates & States

In the middle of a sentence, put a comma after the year and after the state too!

✓ Correct: On May 12, 2026, we drove to Mobile, Alabama, for the game.
